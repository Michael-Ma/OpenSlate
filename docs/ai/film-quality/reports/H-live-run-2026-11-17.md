# Two real films with skill 2.2.0, recorded from start to finish

2026-11-17. The owner asked for two test films through the whole flow, recorded, with video spend under $5 in total. Run folder: `data/test-runs/20261117-h-films/` (not in git): `studio/` is the side studio's data, `raw/film1` and `raw/film2` hold the full recordings and the run records, `outputs/` holds the finished files.

## Setup

- A side studio on port 5173 with its own data folder, started from a worktree at `main`. The owner's studio on 3001 was not touched.
- Director: Codex · gpt-6.1-sol, the studio's default. The studio pins Codex CLI 0.162.1, which does not offer the GPT 6.2 models, so the Director could not use them. Keyframes: Codex images. Video: Viggle H3 at 1080p. Music: Lyria.
- The lead drove the studio's own UI through a recording browser (1920×1080) and answered every keyframe card with the input the Director suggested.
- Spend limits set before the first message: $2.60 and $2.20.

## What the Director did well

- Its first reply opened with the take in two lines and the two it passed on, then carried on. The idea panel showed the format, the aim, the take, the reading, the rule and the cut list.
- The cut list was written before the shots: ten cuts, four clips, stills for the croissant and the chair.
- Every keyframe card carried a suggestion with a reason ("Preserve the comic framing, croissant state and left-to-right continuity").
- In review it found that the pigeon's head turn is at source frames 66–72, outside the planned ranges, moved the three glances onto it, moved the sound to the frame of contact, read nine cut pairs and listed what is still imperfect (the grip, the croissant's size between setups, soft stills). It bought nothing.
- In the second film it kept to what the brief said: no person, no invented history. Its render review caught that the film was widescreen, tried once more, and then said plainly that it could not deliver.
- When the render failed for a reason outside its plan, it said so and left the plan alone.

## Faults the run found

All three were invisible to the tests and the paper runs.

1. **A still row could not read the picture it holds** (H6). The first film stopped at the render with "Row 1: Input is outside configured local roots" after its four clips were bought. Stills were checked against the upload and provider-output folders; keyframes and shared pictures live in the artifact folder. Fixed; the same plan then rendered with no new spend.
2. **Every vertical code film was rendered widescreen** (H7). Since G1 the export size came from the film's aspect, which a code film never sets. Fixed: the resolution setting decides the size and the plan decides the shape.
3. **The review turn ran out of time, silently** (H7). The limit was 180 seconds. Review turns took 75 to 413 seconds. In the first film the review of the clips was cut off after it had read all four contact sheets, and the film was rendered from the planned ranges with nothing on screen saying so. Fixed: ten minutes by default, and a line in the thread when a review does not finish.

Also fixed: the cut-list check reported "Cuts name undeclared shots" at the first dry run, before the plan had reached its shot declarations (H7).

## What is still weak

- **The second film's idea is mild.** It is a pretty, well-made greeting, and it follows the brief, but "a bud among open flowers" is close to the obvious film. The paper runs found a stronger take for this brief twice (the gardener's bench with one new pot in the gap). One real run is one sample.
- **Five plan versions for a four-cut film.** Two of them were renders the fault made necessary; the review loop still spent about 15 minutes.
- **Turn times.** The first turn takes a little over two minutes with 13,800 words of skill to read; a render review took nearly seven.
- **The Director is on gpt-6.1-sol.** Using GPT 6.2 needs the studio's pinned Codex version raised, which is its own piece of work.
- **Stills are soft when punched in**, as the Director noted.
