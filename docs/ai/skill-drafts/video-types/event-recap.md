# Event recap

## Purpose

Capture what an event was and how it felt, for attendees to share and for others to regret missing. Success: a non-attendee wants to come next time; attendees recognise the moments that mattered.

## Format

- 60–180 s main recap; 15–30 s social teaser; 90 s highlight.
- 16:9 main; 9:16 social.
- Speed matters: recaps posted within days perform better than weeks later.

## Structure

- **Arrival/anticipation → build (people, activities, details) → peak moment → closing image + name/date of next edition.**
- Or follow the event's real chronology: doors open → sessions → peak → close.
- Interleave 2–4 short soundbites from attendees or speakers if supplied.

## Scenes and shots

- 90 s: 4–6 scenes (arrival, venue, content, social, peak, close), 18–30 shots.
- Shot lengths 1.5–3 s in montage; soundbites 4–8 s.
- Plan 6–10 specific moments in advance; everything else is b-roll.

## Pacing

- Energetic, cut to music. Slow down for the emotional peak and the final image.

## Must-have shots

- Venue establishing (exterior, signage, wide room).
- Crowd energy: wide audience, reactions, applause.
- Speakers/performers at key moments.
- Details: badges, food, décor, merch, branding.
- Candid interactions: conversations, laughter, handshakes (wide).
- Peak moment: the keynote reveal, the headliner, the award.
- Closing wide or sunset image.

## What matters most

- Truth: it must look like the event that happened.
- Emotion and energy.
- Brand and sponsor visibility where required.

## Narration, text and music

- Usually music-led; optional short attendee soundbites from real recordings.
- Event name, date, location, next-edition date as finishing text.
- Licensed upbeat track; plan cuts against its structure.

## Common mistakes

- Chronological dump with no peak.
- Only wide crowd shots, no faces or details.
- Too long.
- Generic generated crowds that do not resemble the real event.

## Generation caveats

- A recap documents a real event. Generated footage cannot depict what actually happened; build from supplied photos and footage (animate supplied stills gently as keyframes) and use generation only for transitions, atmosphere and abstract inserts.
- Do not generate identifiable attendees, speakers or performers. Do not invent moments.
- Crowds, dancing and applause are fragile in motion; keep motion subtle on supplied crowd photos (slow push, parallax, light).
- Signage, sponsor logos and stage screens: use the supplied photos; do not regenerate text.
- If the event has not happened yet, this is a [trailer](trailer-teaser.md), not a recap.

## Intake questions

1. Which event, when and where? Please attach the best photos and any footage or recorded soundbites.
2. What were the 3–5 moments that mattered most?
3. Length and platforms? (Default: 90 s 16:9 plus a 20 s 9:16 teaser.)
4. Required sponsor/brand mentions and the next-edition date, if any.
