# Social vertical short

Usually a format modifier on another spine (ad, tutorial, story, recap). Load the spine file for structure; apply this file for hook, framing, captions and loop. Use it as the spine only for native-feeling posts: trends, POV skits, reveals, "things I wish I knew", day-in-the-life.

## Purpose

Stop the scroll and hold attention to the end, on a phone, often muted. Success: the viewer stays past the first seconds, watches to the end or rewatches, and the message lands without sound.

## Format

- 9:16, 1080×1920. Platforms accept up to minutes (YouTube Shorts up to 3 min; Reels and TikTok longer), but most effective posts run 7–30 s; ads 9–20 s.
- UI overlays cover the top, bottom and right edge; keep faces, product and text inside the centre safe zone. Leave roughly the bottom fifth and right sixth free.

## Structure

- **Hook (0–2 s) → payoff promise → body → payoff → loop or CTA.** TikTok advises stating the proposition within the first 3 s and keeping the hook within the first 6 s.
- Hook types: a visual surprise, a bold claim, a question, a mid-action start, a result shown first, "POV:" framing.
- Body patterns: list (3 items), before/after, reveal, mini-story, transformation, myth vs fact.
- End on a frame that cuts cleanly back to the first for a loop, or a CTA that fits the platform (follow, comment, link).

## Scenes and shots

- 15 s: 4–8 shots. 30 s: 8–14 shots. Often one location; scenes are beats of the list or story.
- Shot lengths 1–3 s; the hook shot can be under 2 s. Without trimming, use 2–4 shots of 6 s with strong in-shot motion and a reveal inside each.

## Pacing

- Fast, with a pattern change every 2–4 s (new angle, new scale, new reveal) to reset attention.
- No dead air at the start; no fade-in. Start mid-motion.

## Must-have shots

- A hook frame that reads as a thumbnail at small size: one subject, big, high contrast.
- Close-ups (faces and objects fill the vertical frame better than wides).
- The payoff/reveal shot.
- A loop-able final frame or CTA frame with safe-zone space.

## What matters most

- First 2 seconds.
- Native feel: looks like it belongs in the feed (direct-to-camera energy, handheld feel, POV), not a letterboxed TV ad.
- Sound-off readability: captions for any speech.

## Narration, text and music

- Captions on every spoken word (finishing text, inside safe zone). Hook text line in the first frame.
- Voiceover fast and conversational, about 2.5–3 words per second.
- Trending sounds are licensed per platform; plan the cut so music can be swapped. Do not depend on a copyrighted track.

## Common mistakes

- Repurposed horizontal video with tiny subject.
- Slow logo or title intro.
- Text placed under the UI.
- Long setup before the payoff.
- Too many ideas per post.

## Generation caveats

- Generate keyframes at 9:16 natively; do not crop 16:9 frames unless the composition was planned for it.
- Direct-to-camera talking heads need lip sync the model does not reliably provide; use voiceover over action, text-led formats, or POV shots without visible speech.
- Hook text and captions must be added in finishing; Film has no caption layer yet, so record it in `story` as a finishing gap.
- Real creators and influencers: do not synthesise a real person. Use invented personas, labelled as AI where the platform requires realistic synthetic content to be disclosed.
- Fast pattern changes increase shot count and continuity risk; keep one subject and location reference across the post.

## Intake questions

1. Which platform(s), and is this organic or a paid ad? (Default: organic, TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 20 s.)
2. What is the hook and the one takeaway?
3. On-camera persona, voiceover, or text-only?
4. Any trend, sound or format you want to follow?
