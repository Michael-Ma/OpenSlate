# Fashion / lookbook film

## Purpose

Show garments and accessories so that viewers want them: how they look, move and feel, inside a mood. Success: each look reads clearly (silhouette, colour, fabric, detail), and the collection feels like one world.

## Format

- Lookbook loop per look: 6–15 s. Collection film: 30–90 s. Campaign film: 60–180 s.
- 9:16 and 4:5 for social and e-commerce; 16:9 or wider for campaign films. Full-length looks favour vertical.

## Structure

- **Lookbook**: look 1 → look 2 → … each as a mini-sequence (establish, detail, motion), with a consistent location or rotating set.
- **Fashion film**: mood or narrative device (a walk through a city, a ritual, a dream) that carries the looks, ending on a hero look.
- **Campaign**: hook image → movement sequences → hero look → brand.

## Scenes and shots

- Per look: 3 shots — establishing full-length (garment fully visible), detail close-up (fabric, hardware, embroidery, accessory), motion shot (walk, turn, gesture).
- 8 looks × 3 shots = 24 shots ≈ 60–90 s. A 30 s film: 3–4 looks.
- Hero motion shots up to 6 s; details 2–4 s.

## Pacing

- Rhythmic and confident; match cuts on movement between looks.
- Let the full-length shot hold until the eye has scanned the whole silhouette.

## Must-have shots

- Full-length head-to-toe for every look, garment unobstructed.
- Detail macro: weave, texture, buttons, stitching, print.
- Motion that sells drape: walk, turn, wind, a sleeve falling.
- Back or side view when the design has back interest.
- Accessories and footwear inserts.
- Hero closing look.

## What matters most

- Garment fidelity: cut, colour, print, fabric behaviour.
- Fabric in motion (drape, weight, sheen).
- Consistent model, styling and grade across looks.

## Narration, text and music

- Usually music only; sound design of fabric, steps and jewellery adds tactility.
- Look numbers, product names and prices as finishing text, typically small and consistent.
- Music sets attitude; cut to its pulse.

## Common mistakes

- Cropping off feet or hemline in the establishing shot.
- Moody lighting that hides colour and texture.
- Models who change face between looks.
- Too much camera motion; viewers cannot read the garment.

## Generation caveats

- Garment fidelity requires reference images of each piece (front, back, side, fabric close-up, on-model). Condition every shot on them; do not let the model redesign seams, prints or hardware.
- Fabric physics: silk can read plastic, denim painted-on, knits as felt. Describe fabric weight and how it moves; keep motion to one simple movement (a turn, a few steps, wind). Inspect returned clips for warping prints.
- Walking gait and turns over 6 s can deform legs and hems; prefer a few steps or a pivot, cut on motion.
- Hands touching garments (adjusting a collar, opening a bag) are fragile; keep them brief or out of frame.
- Logos, prints with text and monograms will distort in motion; keep them from references and still, or frame details as stills with camera motion only.
- Model identity: use one locked invented model (or supplied model images with consent) across looks.

## Intake questions

1. Which pieces/looks, in what order? Please attach product and on-model images for each.
2. Where will it play and at what length/aspect? (Default: 45 s 9:16, 3–4 looks.)
3. One model or several, and what setting or mood?
4. Are product names or prices shown?
