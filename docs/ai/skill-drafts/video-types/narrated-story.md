# Narrated story / faceless channel

Voice-led videos where narration carries the content and images illustrate it: history, mysteries, legends, science facts, true-crime overviews, "top 10" lists, philosophy, story time. Common for AI production because no on-camera presenter is needed.

## Purpose

Hold attention with a told story or a sequence of facts, using images that make each line vivid. Success: viewers stay to the end; each line has a matching image; facts are correct.

## Format

- Shorts: 30–90 s 9:16. Long-form: 5–15 min 16:9 (high shot count; plan in chapters).
- 16:9 long-form; 9:16 shorts.

## Structure

- **Cold-open question or striking claim → context → escalation (each chapter raises a new question) → reveal/answer → reflection or hook to next video.**
- Lists: hook → items in ascending interest → best item last → outro.
- Open loops: pose a question early and answer it late.

## Scenes and shots

- One scene per chapter or list item. One shot per narration sentence or two.
- 60 s short: 10–15 shots. 10 min: 100–150 shots; plan chapter by chapter and reuse locations and characters.
- Shot length follows narration (4–6 s); slow push/parallax on illustrative stills is acceptable.

## Pacing

- Paced by voice. Change image on each new idea; hold on reveals.
- Every 20–40 s, a visual change of register (map, close-up, reconstruction, object) to reset attention.

## Must-have shots

- Opening image that embodies the hook.
- Establishing shots for each era/place.
- Recurring figures (if any) with locked references.
- Objects/evidence close-ups.
- Maps or diagrams (composed for finishing labels).
- Reveal image.

## What matters most

- Script quality and factual accuracy.
- Image-to-line correspondence.
- Consistent look across the whole video.

## Narration, text and music

- Narration is the spine: 140–160 wpm; 60 s ≈ 150 words. Write and settle the script before shot planning.
- Dates, names and place labels as finishing text. Captions for shorts.
- Music bed by mood per chapter; ducked under voice.

## Common mistakes

- Images that are generic or contradict the line.
- Fabricated facts or invented quotes.
- Monotone visual rhythm for minutes.
- Overlong intro.

## Generation caveats

- Historical and real figures: depict as clearly illustrative (painterly, silhouettes, period figures without claimed likeness) unless working from public-domain portraits; never generate realistic footage that could pass as a real recording of a real person or event, and label realistic reconstructions.
- Period accuracy: specify era, region, clothing and architecture; inspect for anachronisms.
- Maps, dates and documents do not render reliably; compose and add labels in finishing.
- Long reconstructions of action (battles, escapes) must be split into single actions per shot.
- Violence and true crime: depict implication, not graphic detail; do not depict real victims.

## Intake questions

1. Topic, angle and audience? Do you have a script or sources? (Default: I will draft a script and flag facts for you to verify.)
2. Short or long-form, and length? (Default: 60 s 9:16.)
3. Narrator voice: your recording or generated? Language?
4. Visual approach: realistic reconstructions, illustrated, or archival-style?
