# Product showcase / demo

## Purpose

Show what a physical product looks like, how it is used and why it is better, closely enough that a viewer can judge it. Success: the viewer could describe the product's form, scale, materials and main use after one watch, and nothing shown differs from the real item.

## Format

- Product-page loop or hero: 10–30 s. Feature demo: 30–90 s. Marketplace listings commonly 15–60 s.
- 1:1 or 4:5 for product pages and feeds; 16:9 for web hero and YouTube; 9:16 for social.
- Often muted and looping; design so the first and last frame can join.

## Structure

- **Reveal → details → in use → result → packshot.**
- For demos: hook (the problem or the result first) → reveal → 2–4 features, each shown, not told → result → CTA.
- One feature per scene. Order features by importance to the buyer, not by spec sheet.

## Scenes and shots

- 20 s loop: 1–2 scenes, 4–6 shots. 60 s demo: 3–5 scenes, 10–16 shots.
- Per feature: a context shot (product in its setting) + a detail close-up + a use or result shot.
- Shot lengths 2–4 s; hero orbit up to 6 s.

## Pacing

- Calm and precise for premium goods; brisk for gadgets and consumables.
- Let each detail shot hold long enough to register texture (at least 2 s).

## Must-have shots

- Hero: whole product, clean background or setting, three-quarter angle, label forward.
- Scale shot: product beside a hand, body or familiar object.
- Material/detail macro: texture, finish, stitching, interface, mechanism.
- In-use shot in a realistic context.
- Result or benefit shot (the clean surface, the cooked food, the organised bag).
- Packshot end frame, with space for name and price/CTA added in finishing.

## What matters most

- Fidelity: shape, proportions, colours, materials, labels, number of buttons/ports.
- Lighting that reveals form (rim light, soft key, controlled reflections).
- Honest scale and honest results.

## Narration, text and music

- Usually music only, with feature callouts as finishing text (one short phrase per feature).
- Demo voiceover: describe the benefit while the picture shows the feature; 2–3 short sentences per feature.
- Music: clean, rhythmic, unobtrusive.

## Common mistakes

- Product only in empty void; no context or scale.
- Showing features in the order of the spec sheet.
- Fast spins that blur the details buyers want to see.
- Generated product variants that do not exist (wrong colour, extra button).

## Generation caveats

- Condition every shot on product photos from the matching angle. If only one angle is supplied, keep the camera near that angle; do not orbit to unseen sides. Ask for more angles if the plan needs them.
- Prefer camera motion (slow push-in, slight arc, parallax) and light motion over rotating the product. A product turntable spin often reshapes it.
- Labels and printed UI: keep them from the reference and keep them large; do not prompt for new text. Small print and screens need finishing or should be framed out.
- Hands operating buttons, zips, lids and cables are fragile. Show the before and after states in two shots, or one simple press. Inspect hand shots closely.
- Physical results (a stain disappearing, hair changing) are claims; show only what the person confirms is accurate.
- Liquids and powders: one simple, slow motion per shot.

## Intake questions

1. Which product, and which variant/colour? Please attach photos from front, side, back and detail angles plus the logo.
2. Where will this play (product page, ad, social), at what length and aspect? (Default: 20 s 1:1 loop.)
3. What are the top 2–3 features or benefits, in priority order?
4. Should it show the product in use by a person, or the product alone?
