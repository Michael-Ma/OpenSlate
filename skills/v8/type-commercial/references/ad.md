# Commercial / ad

## Purpose

Change one behaviour: buy, sign up, visit, book, remember the brand. Success: the viewer knows within seconds what is being sold and by whom, feels one clear benefit, and knows the next step.

## Format

- Standard lengths: 6 s bumper, 15 s, 30 s, 60 s. Social ads often 9–30 s.
- 16:9 for TV/YouTube in-stream; 9:16 for Reels/Stories/TikTok/Shorts; 4:5 for feeds; 1:1 for some placements. Ask where it runs; plan for crop if more than one.
- Most feed placements autoplay muted; YouTube is mostly sound-on. Design to work both ways.

## Structure

Pick one; state it in the story:

- **ABCD** (YouTube/Google): Attention in the first seconds, Branding early and often, Connection (emotion, story, benefit), Direction (clear CTA).
- **Problem → solution → proof → CTA** for direct response.
- **AIDA**: attention, interest, desire, action.
- **Mini-story**: situation, turn the product causes, payoff, brand.
- 15 s: hook (0–3 s) → benefit/demo (3–11 s) → brand + CTA (11–15 s). 30 s: hook → problem or setup → product in action → payoff → brand/CTA. 6 s: one image, one idea, the brand.

## Scenes and shots

- 15 s: 1–2 scenes, 4–7 shots. 30 s: 2–4 scenes, 7–12 shots. 60 s: 3–6 scenes, 12–20 shots.
- Shot lengths 3–4 s in the body (the shortest a shot can be), 4–6 s for the hero product shot and end card.
- Each shot is at least 3 s (90 frames); Film cuts every clip to its `desiredFrames`. A 30 s spot is 7–9 shots at 3–4 s; give the body visible motion and save longer holds for the hero and end card.

## Pacing

- Strongest image first. No logo-only openings, no slow fade-in.
- Product or brand visible by about the 5 s mark in a 15–30 s spot.
- Accelerate into the payoff; hold the end card long enough to read (at least 2 s).

## Must-have shots

- Hook shot: motion, surprise, or a relatable problem in the first frame.
- Product hero: clean, well-lit, correct, label facing camera.
- Product in use or the benefit made visible (before/after, the result, the reaction).
- Human moment: face reacting, hand using, the emotional payoff.
- Brand/packshot end frame with negative space for logo and CTA.

## What matters most

- One message. If it cannot be said in one sentence, cut.
- Product fidelity and branding: correct product, correct colours, recognisable at a glance.
- Sound-off legibility: the story reads from picture plus planned captions.

## Narration, text and music

- Voiceover optional; often 30–45 words in 15 s is too many, aim for 25–35. For 30 s, 55–70 words.
- CTA and brand name: say them and show them. Plan supers and end-card copy as finishing text, not generated pixels.
- Music sets energy and should hit a beat at the product reveal and the end card. Leave room for a sonic logo if the brand has one.

## Common mistakes

- Burying the product until the last 3 s.
- Several benefits, several CTAs.
- Claims the visuals cannot support, or invented results, specs or prices.
- Copy-heavy frames that cannot be read on a phone.
- A logo intro that viewers skip.

## Generation caveats

- Product drift (shape, label, cap, colour) is the top failure. Condition every product frame on the supplied product image; keep the product still and let light, camera or environment move. Avoid hands rotating, opening or squeezing it unless that action is simple and inspected.
- Logos and packaging text: never trust generated lettering. Keep the real label facing camera from the reference, large and flat; or compose with clean space and finish the logo outside generation.
- Real people and endorsements: no real or lookalike celebrities, no fake customers presented as real. Use clearly fictional talent; label realistic synthetic people where the platform requires it.
- Demonstrations: a generated demo is a depiction. Do not show performance the product cannot deliver; regulated categories (health, finance, cosmetics results) need claim review by the person.
- Liquids, food, cosmetics textures: keep motion slow and simple (a pour, a drip, steam); avoid complex splashes in one shot.

## Intake questions

1. What exactly is being sold, and what is the single benefit or offer? (Default: the main benefit in your brief.)
2. Where will it run, and at what lengths/aspects? (Default: 30 s 16:9 plus a 9:16 crop plan.)
3. What is the call to action and the end-card copy?
4. Do you have product images, logo files and brand colours? Any claims we must or must not make?
