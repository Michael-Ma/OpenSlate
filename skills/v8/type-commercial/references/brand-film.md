# Brand film

## Purpose

Make viewers feel what a company stands for, so they remember and prefer it, without a hard sell. Success: the viewer can say the brand's belief in their own words and associates a feeling and an image with it.

## Format

- 60–180 s for the hero film; 15–30 s cut-downs for paid media.
- 16:9 (or wider, letterboxed) for web, events and YouTube; 9:16 and 4:5 cut-downs for social.

## Structure

- **Manifesto**: tension in the world → the brand's belief → proof in people and actions → the promise → brand.
- **Origin**: why it started → what it fought → what it does now → what it means for you.
- **Anthem**: a rhythmic sequence of moments that each embody one value, building to the brand line.
- End on the brand line and logo, not on an offer.

## Scenes and shots

- 90 s: 4–6 movements (scenes), 18–28 shots. A movement is a value or a turn in the argument, not a location.
- Shot lengths 2–5 s; key emotional images 5–6 s.

## Pacing

- Build. Start quieter and more intimate, widen and accelerate towards the belief statement, then hold the final image.
- Cut on rhythm with the music or on the voiceover's phrasing.

## Must-have shots

- An opening image that states the tension or the world.
- People: faces, gestures, work, community; the brand is shown through people affected by it.
- Recurring visual motif (a colour, an object, a gesture) that ties the film together and returns at the end.
- Craft/process close-ups if the brand makes things.
- Product or service presence that is felt rather than sold (in the scene, not in a packshot), except in the end frame.
- Final emblem shot leading into logo and brand line.

## What matters most

- One belief, said plainly. Everything else is evidence.
- Emotional authenticity and specificity; generic stock-like moments kill brand films.
- A distinctive and consistent look (see visual styles).

## Narration, text and music

- Usually led by voiceover or a manifesto read: 120–150 wpm, short lines, room to breathe. A 90 s film carries 150–190 words.
- Text: sparse; a few key words or the brand line, as finishing text.
- Music is the engine; choose its arc first and plan cuts against it.

## Common mistakes

- Abstract claims ("we innovate") with no concrete images.
- A brochure of features.
- Stock-like diversity montages without specificity.
- A logo that appears only once, at the end, so the film could be anyone's.

## Generation caveats

- Real employees, founders and customers should come from supplied footage or photos with consent. Do not synthesise a real founder or present invented people as real customers or staff.
- Logos and brand marks in the world (signage, uniforms, packaging): keep them large and inspect, or finish outside generation.
- Montages of many different people multiply identity risk only if people recur; if a character recurs, lock a reference.
- Crowds, sport and dance: keep to simple, one-direction motion per shot; avoid complex choreography in a single 6 s clip.
- Avoid depicting real places, events or achievements as documentary fact if they are generated.

## Intake questions

1. What does the brand believe, in one sentence, and who needs to hear it? (Default: I will draft from your brief.)
2. Where will it be used, at what length? (Default: 90 s 16:9 with a 30 s cut-down.)
3. Voiceover manifesto, real voices, or music only?
4. Do you have brand guidelines, logo files, and real people or locations we must show?
