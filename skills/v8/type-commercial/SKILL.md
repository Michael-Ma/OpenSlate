# Commercial

Routed for `filmKind.kind: "commercial"`. Read the subtype's reference before planning: [ad](references/ad.md) (`ad`, `ugc_ad`), [brand film](references/brand-film.md) (`brand_film`), [app promo](references/app-promo.md) (`app_promo`). Authority and tools are in core.

## Shared shape

- **One claim** (the single-minded proposition), said in one sentence; if it needs two, cut one.
- **Proof** the picture can carry: the product in use, the result, a human reaction. Generated visuals are depictions; never show results, specs, prices or customers that are not real.
- **Close**: brand or packshot frame with clean space for the logo and call to action, held at least 2 s. The CTA and any copy are finishing text, not generated pixels.
- Strongest image first; product or brand visible by about 5 s in a 15–30 s spot.

## Shots

Must-haves: a hook shot, the product hero (clean, correct, label facing camera, conditioned on the supplied product image), the product in use or the benefit made visible, a human moment, the end frame. Shot lengths 3–5 s for most of the body. 15 s: 3–5 shots; 30 s: 6–9.

## Fidelity

- Product drift is the top failure: condition every product frame on the supplied image, keep the product still and move light, camera or environment.
- No real or lookalike celebrities, no fake testimonials, no competitor brands. UGC-style ads use clearly invented people, labelled where the platform requires.
- Regulated categories (health, finance, cosmetics results): flag claims for the person's review.

## Funny ads

When `tones` includes `comedy`, read `craft-comedy`: the joke must serve the claim, and the product is the punch or the payoff, never the butt.

## Story and style

Story unit: claim and proof. Frameworks: problem → solution → proof → CTA, ABCD, mini-story. Style: `glossy-commercial` or `grounded-realism`; never `watercolor-ink` or `pixel-art` with a real product.

## Intake

What is sold and its one benefit; where it runs and at which lengths and aspects; the CTA; product images, logo and brand colours; claims that must or must not appear. Ask only what the brief does not answer, in one compact message with defaults.
