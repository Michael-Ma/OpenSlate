# Mini-doc

## Purpose

Tell a true story about a real person, place, craft, organisation or issue. Success: the viewer trusts what they saw, learns something specific, and feels the subject's stakes.

## Format

- 2–8 min for web/YouTube; 60–90 s social versions.
- 16:9; 9:16 for social cuts with centre-safe composition.

## Structure

- **Character → world → tension or question → journey → reflection.**
- Open on a strong observational moment or a striking line from the subject, then establish who and where.
- The tension can be small (will the bread rise, will the shop survive), but there must be one.
- End on a quiet image or line that leaves meaning, not a summary.

## Scenes and shots

- 3 min: 5–8 scenes, 30–45 shots. Scenes are chapters: place, work, history, problem, turning point, now.
- Each scene: establishing wide, observational medium shots, detail inserts (hands at work, objects, textures), and portrait shots.
- Shot lengths 3–6 s; observational moments can hold the full generated length.

## Pacing

- Patient. Let moments breathe; cut on content of the voice, not on beats.
- Alternate interview/voice sections with pure picture sections.

## Must-have shots

- Establishing shots of place at different times or weather.
- Subject portrait: a still, direct, held shot.
- Process/work details: the subject's craft or daily routine.
- Archival or evidence shots (supplied photos, documents).
- Environmental b-roll for transitions.
- Final image of the subject in their world.

## What matters most

- Truthfulness. The real subject's words and supplied media are primary; generated images are illustration.
- Specific details over generic beauty.
- Voice: the subject's own words where possible.

## Narration, text and music

- Best carried by the subject's supplied recording or interview; otherwise a measured narrator at 130–150 wpm.
- Lower thirds (name, role, place, date) as finishing text.
- Music sparing; silence and ambient sound add authenticity.

## Common mistakes

- Generated images passed off as real footage of the real subject.
- Narrator explaining everything instead of letting the subject speak.
- No tension; a sequence of nice shots.
- Over-scoring with emotional music.

## Generation caveats

- Real people: do not generate an identifiable real subject unless they supplied reference and consented, and never fabricate their statements. Prefer the person's own photos and footage, or depict them indirectly (hands, silhouette, back, objects, places).
- Mark generated reconstructions as illustrative (finishing text or narration) when the context implies documentary truth; platforms require labels for realistic synthetic depictions of real events.
- Real places: generated versions will not match the real location; use supplied photos as keyframe references and avoid claims of exact likeness.
- Archival material: animate supplied photos gently (slow push, parallax) rather than regenerating them; do not alter documentary content.
- Text in documents, signs and newspapers will not reproduce; use the supplied scan as a still frame.

## Intake questions

1. Who or what is the subject, and do you have their consent and their own photos, footage or recordings?
2. What is the question or tension the film follows?
3. Is there an interview recording or transcript to build from? (Default: narrator script drafted from your notes, marked as draft.)
4. Length and destination? (Default: 3 min 16:9.)
