# Performance: dance and choreography

Read when a shot shows solo or group dance. Structure and ratio come from music video.

## Purpose in the edit

Dance turns the beat into bodies. It carries choruses, post-choruses and drops, and it is the main escalation tool in pop and idol videos: more dancers, bigger moves, larger spaces. The viewer must be able to read the shape of the move. A generic sway in every shot is the failure this guide prevents: each dance shot has a specific move, a specific scale and a specific job.

## Design the moves first

Before writing prompts, name a small move vocabulary for the song in `story`: a signature hook move for the chorus (repeated and escalated), two or three verse grooves, and one or two drop hits. Describe each in plain words a model understands (a sharp arm point on the beat, a slow body roll, a single spin, a hair whip, a jump and land, shoulder bounce, a step-touch side to side, a freeze). Reuse the signature move in every chorus with a change of scale, place or number of dancers.

## Coverage and shot list template

Solo, per setup:

1. **Full figure master.** Head to feet with floor room. Shows the whole phrase.
2. **Medium wide.** Knees up; upper-body moves and face.
3. **Close.** Face and shoulders for attitude, eyes to camera on hits.
4. **Body inserts.** Feet, hands, hips, hair. Short, on hits.
5. **Low wide.** Camera low for power on jumps and stomps.
6. **Silhouette or backlit.** Shape only; safe for fast moves.

Group, per setup, add:

7. **Formation wide.** All dancers, front or high angle; shows the pattern.
8. **Leader single.** The lead in front, others soft behind.
9. **Line or diagonal.** Long lens down a line of dancers; compression makes unison read.
10. **Top shot.** Overhead on a formation change.

Use 1, 7 and 9 for the signature move, 2–4 and 8 between hits, 5, 6 and 10 for drops and section changes.

## Framing and lenses

- Always have at least one full-figure shot per dance section; never crop feet on footwork.
- 24–35 mm for full figure in a room and for energy; 50 mm for medium; 85–135 mm along lines of dancers.
- Wide-angle close to the dancer exaggerates limbs; use it only for deliberate distortion.
- Leave space in the direction of travel and above the head for jumps.

## Camera movement

- When the dancer moves fast, keep the camera still or slow. When the dancer holds, move the camera.
- Do not mirror the dancer's motion; move around the space at a different rhythm.
- Good moves: slow push-in to a freeze, lateral track with a travelling dancer, slow orbit around a held pose, crane up on a formation, a whip pan that hides a cut.
- One dominant move per shot.

## Motion-prompt vocabulary

Describe one move phrase per shot, its quality and its end state:

- "hits a sharp arm point on the beat and holds", "slow fluid body roll from chest to hips", "spins once and lands facing camera", "jumps and lands in a crouch", "steps side to side with a relaxed groove", "dancers move in unison, then freeze", "the line ripples from left to right", "hair whips as she turns her head".
- Quality words: sharp, snappy, staccato; fluid, liquid, slow; heavy, grounded; light, bouncy.
- Speed: "in slow motion" for drops and hits; slow motion reads as intentional and hides mismatch with the tempo.
- End with the pose: "ends in a still pose, arms raised" gives the edit a clean cut point.

## What models get wrong and mitigations

| Problem | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fast choreography melts: extra limbs, limbs passing through bodies, twisted joints | One simple move per shot; 2–3 s used window; slow motion for complex moves; silhouette or motion blur for fast ones; cut to inserts. |
| Feet sliding, floating, floor contact lost | Keyframe the feet planted and visible on a textured floor; prefer upper-body moves for medium shots; low camera on jumps with a clear landing. |
| Moves off the beat (the model does not hear the song) | Choose moves whose timing can be trimmed: hits followed by holds, sways, slow motion. Cut on the visual hit; let the edit retime slightly. |
| Generic dance: the same sway every shot | Use the named move vocabulary; vary scale and angle; reserve the signature move for choruses. |
| Group unison breaks, dancers duplicate or merge | Three to five dancers in one keyframe; state the formation; distinct wardrobe accents per dancer; long lens from front; more dancers only in backlit wides. |
| Cloned faces in a group | Lead sharp, others soft focus, turned or lit from behind; per-dancer references when faces matter. |
| Partner contact (lifts, holds, spins in pairs) tangles | Avoid lifts; show the moment before and after contact; use silhouettes. |
| Spins change identity or wardrobe | Half spins; ending facing camera; reference on every shot. |

## Continuity

- Fix wardrobe, shoes and hair per section; footwear must stay the same across a section.
- Keep the formation and each dancer's position in `story` as a simple stage plan (lead center, two left, two right). Keep screen direction of travel consistent across cuts.
- Match the pose at a cut: end shot A on a hold, start shot B on the same hold from a new angle.
- Escalate by section: solo in chorus 1, small group in chorus 2, full group or new space in the final chorus.
