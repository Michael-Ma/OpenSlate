# Writing narration by type

## Explainer and tutorial

- ABT in words: the situation, the problem, the resolution. One step per shot.
- Name the step, then let the picture show it: "Fold it once." over the folding.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over adjectives; no rhetorical questions in a 30 s piece.
- About 150 words per minute for a 60 s piece; read it aloud against the shot list.

## Commercial

- 15 s: 25–35 words; 30 s: 55–70 words. Say the brand and the call to action and show them (the text itself is finishing work).
- One benefit. The voice states the claim; the pictures are the proof.
- Never voice a claim the person did not supply (results, prices, guarantees).

## Narrative and kids

- Often no narration at all; let action tell the story.
- A storyteller voice for kids: short sentences, repetition, a refrain the child can predict, about 2 words per second.
- Inner voice for a character: first person, present tense, one line per beat.

## Narrated story and documentary

- The voice leads; every shot is evidence for the sentence over it.
- Open with the question or the mystery in the first sentence.
- For lists, number the items in the voice ("Number three…") because on-screen numbers are not available.
- Documentary: use the person's own interview words when supplied; never write words for a real person.

## Social and comedy

- At most one voice line in a native post: the setup ("Just one. I'm only getting one."), a deadpan announcement, or a device voice ("Unexpected item in the bagging area."). Never explain the joke.
- Put it in the setup shot; let the punch play without words.
- A voice that contradicts the picture is a joke engine: earnest voice, absurd image.

## Trailer

- Sparse: three to five short lines across the piece, rising in stakes.
- The last line before the title is the question the film answers.

## Checks

- Read the script at the target rate against the shot lengths; every line ends inside its shot.
- No line repeats what the frame shows.
- Facts, names and pronunciations match the brief; unknowns are marked for the person.
