# Narrated story

Routed for `filmKind.kind: "narrated_story"`. Read [the guide](references/guide.md) before planning. Narration follows `craft-narration`. Authority and tools are in core.

## Shape

- Open with the question, mystery or promise in the first sentence and the first image.
- `history` and `legend`: ABT or a chronological spine with one turn; each section answers part of the question.
- `list`: weakest to strongest, the last item calling back to the first; number items in the voice.
- Write the script first, then cut shots to its sentences: one sentence, one shot, about 2.5 words per second.

## Shots

Every shot is evidence for its sentence: a place, an object, a reconstructed moment, a map-like composition. Pictures are never decoration. Generated reconstructions of real events or people are depictions; never present them as archival footage, and never generate an identifiable real person.

## Facts

Keep names, dates and figures exactly as supplied; mark what you could not verify and say so once. Do not invent quotes.

## Platforms

Faceless channels face monetisation rules against repetitive, mass-produced content: make each piece original in script and imagery.

## Story and style

Story unit: the section or list item. Core: the question. Frameworks: ABT, countdown list. Style: `vintage-film-16mm`, `noir-bw` for history; `watercolor-ink`, `paper-cutout` for legends; `grounded-realism` for science and facts.

## Intake

Topic, angle and audience; a supplied script or sources; voice (stock or the person's own); length and platform when unknown.
