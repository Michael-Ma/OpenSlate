# Testimonial / interview-style

## Purpose

Let a real customer, user or participant vouch for an experience in their own words. Success: a prospective customer sees someone like them describe a specific before, a decision, and a measurable after, and believes it.

## Format

- 45–90 s main version; 15–30 s highlight cuts for social; 2–3 min case study.
- 16:9 for web and LinkedIn/YouTube; 9:16 and 1:1 for social cuts.

## Structure

- **Who they are → the problem before → why they chose this → what changed (specific results) → recommendation.**
- Open on the strongest line (often the result or the emotional moment), then go back to the beginning.
- Case studies add context (company, scale) and a numbers beat.

## Scenes and shots

- 60 s: 3–5 scenes (intro, before, decision, after, recommendation), 10–16 shots.
- The interview is the spine; b-roll illustrates each claim.
- b-roll shots 2–4 s; interview segments as long as the thought.

## Pacing

- Paced by speech. Cut to b-roll on each concrete noun or claim; return to the speaker for emotion and conviction.

## Must-have shots

- The speaker on camera (from their own supplied footage) or a respectful indirect substitute.
- Their environment: workplace, home, shop.
- Before-state illustration.
- The product or service in their actual use.
- After-state or result illustration.
- Logo/end frame.

## What matters most

- Authenticity and specificity: real names, real numbers, real words.
- The speaker's credibility (role, context) established early.
- Legal and ethical honesty.

## Narration, text and music

- No narrator; the speaker's voice carries it. Supplied recordings are primary; preserve their words.
- Lower third with name and role; key result numbers as finishing text.
- Music soft and low.

## Common mistakes

- Scripted, generic praise ("it's great, I love it").
- No specific before/after.
- Too much product b-roll, not enough person.
- Fabricating or embellishing results.

## Generation caveats

- **Do not fabricate testimonials.** Generated people presented as real customers, or invented reviews, are deceptive and, in the US, prohibited by the FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials (in effect since October 2024); similar rules exist elsewhere.
- Use the real customer's supplied footage or recording, with consent. Generated imagery may illustrate their story (their workplace, their problem, the product) as b-roll.
- If the person wants a dramatised testimonial, it must be labelled as a dramatisation with an actor or illustrative character, based on a real customer's words with permission. Ask before planning it.
- Lip sync and talking heads from image-to-video are unreliable; do not generate the speaker talking. Use the real footage or indirect visuals under the real audio.
- Results shown visually (charts, before/after) must match what the customer said; numbers as finishing text.

## Intake questions

1. Who is speaking, and do you have their recorded interview or footage and their consent to use it?
2. What specific results did they get (numbers, time saved, what changed)?
3. Where will it be used, and at what lengths? (Default: 60 s 16:9 plus a 20 s cut.)
4. If no real recording exists: do you want a clearly labelled dramatisation, or a different video type (explainer or commercial)?
