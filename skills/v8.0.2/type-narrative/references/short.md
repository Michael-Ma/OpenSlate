# Narrative short film

## Purpose

Tell one story with a character who wants something, meets resistance, and changes or reveals something by the end. Success: the viewer understands who, wants what, and why it matters within the first quarter, and the ending lands as earned rather than explained.

## Format

- 1–5 min is realistic for this pipeline; micro-shorts 30–90 s. Longer work multiplies continuity risk.
- 16:9 or wider (2.39:1 letterbox via composition) for film; 9:16 for social-first micro-drama.

## Structure

- Three acts. Setup (about 25%): world, character, want, inciting change. Confrontation (about 50%): attempts, complications, a midpoint turn, a low point. Resolution (about 25%): decision, climax, final image.
- For under 90 s, compress to situation → turn → payoff. One turn, one payoff.
- Open on an image that states the world or the problem; end on an image that answers or inverts it.

## Scenes and shots

- One scene per location/time change or dramatic beat. 30–90 s micro-short: 2–4 scenes. 3 min: 5–8 scenes.
- 3–8 shots per scene. Build each scene from establishing → coverage (medium, close) → reaction/insert → exit or button.
- A dialogue-free beat needs at least one shot of action and one of reaction; the reaction is where meaning lands.
- 3 min at about 4 s used per shot: 35–45 shots; at 6 s, 25–30 shots and a slower rhythm.

## Pacing

- Let rhythm follow emotion: longer holds for discovery, grief and wonder; shorter shots in conflict and chase.
- Vary scale every cut. Do not cut between two shots of the same size and angle of the same subject.
- Give the climax the most coverage and the ending the longest hold.

## Must-have shots

- Opening world/establishing image.
- Character introduction: clear face or silhouette, distinctive costume, in a pose that shows their want.
- Inciting incident: the change made visible.
- Reaction close-ups at each turn.
- Climax action broken into start, peak and result shots.
- Final image that mirrors or answers the opening.

## What matters most

- Character consistency across every shot: face, hair, costume, props, scale.
- Clear screen direction and eyelines so the geography reads (keep the 180° line).
- Visual storytelling: the story should survive with sound off.

## Narration, text and music

- Prefer no narration; show it. Use voiceover only for an intentional voice (diary, fable, memory), written sparsely.
- Dialogue lip sync is a weak point of image-to-video; stage dialogue off-face (backs, wide shots, over-shoulder, cutaways) or replace it with voiceover or silence.
- Title card at start or end only. Music carries emotion; plan where it drops out (the turn) and returns (the payoff).

## Common mistakes

- Too much plot for the runtime; three subplots in two minutes.
- A character who wants nothing; scenes that are pretty but do not change the situation.
- Every shot a wide establishing shot; no close-ups or reactions.
- Explaining the ending in voiceover.
- Changing the character's look between scenes.

## Generation caveats

- Identity drift: lock a character reference sheet (front, three-quarter, full body, costume) before shot planning; reuse it for every keyframe. Name the character with the same descriptors in every prompt. Keep cast to 1–3 recurring characters.
- Two characters interacting physically (hug, fight, handshake) often fuse or warp; stage contact in a wide shot or cut around it (before, reaction, after).
- Long action (a chase, a fight, cooking a meal) must be split: one action per 6 s shot, each with its own keyframe start state.
- Hands with props: keep props big and simple; avoid writing, typing, phone screens.
- Text in world (letters, signs, screens) will not read; show the reader's reaction, or note an insert to be finished outside generation.
- Real people: do not cast recognisable real people or celebrities.

## Intake questions

1. What happens, in one sentence: who wants what, and what stands in the way? (Default: I will draft a logline from your brief.)
2. How long, and where will it play? (Default: 2 min, 16:9.)
3. Dialogue, voiceover, or silent with music? (Default: silent with music.)
4. Any characters, locations or references already designed?
