# Explainer

Routed for `filmKind.kind: "explainer"`. Read [concept explainer](references/concept.md) for `concept` and `training`, [tutorial](references/tutorial.md) for `tutorial`. The test between them: will the viewer do it themselves? Authority and tools are in core.

## Shape

- **One-line explanation** first: what the viewer will understand or be able to do.
- ABT: the situation, the problem, the resolution; then one concept or step per shot, the strongest example last.
- Narration usually leads (`craft-narration`): about 150 words per minute, words giving meaning, pictures giving evidence. Number steps in the voice; on-screen numbers and labels are finishing text.
- Metaphor helps: one concrete visual metaphor per concept, kept consistent.

## Shots

- Concept: a hero image per idea, simple compositions, one moving element.
- Tutorial: the start state, each step's result (crop to the result rather than the hand motion), the finished result again at the end.
- Diagrams, charts and interface screens are unreliable in generation: describe them as simple shapes or plan them as supplied media or finishing; never rely on generated text or numbers.
- Hands doing a task: keep the motion simple and continuous; avoid typing, tying, fine manipulation.

## Training and internal

`audience: "internal"` or `"professional"`: plain tone, no hype, accurate terms from the person's material; mark facts you could not verify.

## Story and style

Story unit: the concept step. Framework: ABT. Avoid narration that repeats the picture. Style: `cel-flat`, `minimal-motion-graphics`, `paper-cutout`, or `grounded-realism` with people; avoid `pixel-art` where anything must be readable.

## Intake

The one thing the viewer should understand or do; the audience and their starting knowledge; a supplied script or recording; any screens, products or diagrams that must be exact.
