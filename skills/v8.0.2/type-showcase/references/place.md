# Place film (real estate, travel, hotel, venue)

## Purpose

Make a viewer want to be in a place: buy or rent it, book it, visit it. Success: the viewer understands the layout or geography, the best features, and the feeling of being there, and nothing shown misrepresents the real place.

## Format

- Listing video: 60–120 s (social cut 30–60 s; full tours up to about 3 min). Destination/hotel film: 30–90 s. Venue tour: 60–120 s.
- 16:9 for listings sites and YouTube; 9:16 for social; listing portals vary.

## Structure

- **Real estate**: strongest feature or exterior hook → arrival/approach → follow the natural walking route (entry, living, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, outdoor) → neighbourhood/aerial → details/CTA.
- **Travel/destination**: arrival → sense of place (landscape, streets) → experiences (food, culture, activities) → golden-hour peak → departure/invitation.
- **Hotel/venue**: arrival → signature space → rooms/amenities → experience moments (dining, spa, event) → evening hero → booking CTA.
- Order by flow of movement so the viewer builds a mental map.

## Scenes and shots

- One scene per room or area (real estate) or per experience (travel).
- 90 s listing: 7–10 scenes, 20–30 shots, 2–4 s each; standout rooms get extra.
- Per room: a wide establishing (corner, eye level, verticals straight) + a slow move (push, glide) + 1 detail (fixture, view, material).

## Pacing

- Real estate: smooth and unhurried; long gliding moves, consistent direction.
- Travel: rhythmic, more energy and people, slow at golden hour.

## Must-have shots

- Exterior or arrival hero (twilight or golden hour).
- Aerial/establishing context: plot, neighbourhood, coastline, skyline.
- Wide of each key space, verticals straight, from a corner.
- Best-feature close-up (view, fireplace, kitchen island, pool).
- Transitional threshold shots (through doors, windows) for flow.
- Lifestyle moment (coffee on the terrace, dinner set), people optional.
- Closing hero image.

## What matters most

- Accuracy: layout, finishes, view and condition must match reality.
- Light: bright, natural, consistent white balance.
- Spatial coherence: the route makes sense.

## Narration, text and music

- Optional voiceover (agent or narrator) for key facts; otherwise music with finishing text: address, price, bedrooms, square footage, booking info.
- Travel films: light voiceover or ambient sound plus music.

## Common mistakes

- Random room order; viewers lose orientation.
- Wide-angle distortion and tilted verticals.
- Generic stock interiors.
- Fast whip moves in property tours.

## Generation caveats

- **Do not misrepresent a real property or place.** For a real listing, keyframes must come from the supplied photos of that property; animate them with gentle camera moves (push, glide, parallax) and do not add rooms, furniture, views or finishes that are not there. Virtual staging must be disclosed as such; many listing rules and markets require this.
- Camera moves through geometry the model has not seen invent space. Keep moves short and within the visible photo; do not travel through doorways into unseen rooms.
- For fictional or conceptual places (resort concept, fantasy town), generation is fine; lock the architecture with consistent references.
- Signage, addresses and price text: finishing only.
- Real people at destinations: invented figures only, small in frame, or none.
- Aerials generated from ground photos are invented; for real properties use only supplied aerial images.

## Intake questions

1. Is this a real place? Please attach photos of every space to show (and floor plan or aerial if available).
2. What is the hero feature, and what facts must appear (price, address, size, booking)?
3. Length and destination? (Default: 60 s 16:9 plus a 30 s 9:16 cut.)
4. Voiceover, or music with text?
