# Formats

What the film is and what it is for. Decide this first: the format fixes the length, the picture shape, what the first three seconds must do, whether there is a product, speech or text, how the film ends and its unit of structure. Then decide the aim and read [aims](aims.md). The format gives the slots; the aim's shape fills the turn.

**Reading the format from a brief** that does not name it:

- a product, a price, an offer, "promote", "sell": Advert;
- a brand or a cause with a feeling, "our values", "awareness": Brand film;
- "show off" an object, a dish, a garment or a place, with no claim: Showcase;
- "explain", "how", "why", a tutorial, tips: Explainer;
- a real person, maker, place or event, "true story", "recap": Mini documentary;
- "tell the story of", a legend, a bedtime or scary story told by a voice: Narrated story;
- an episode, 短剧, a CEO, revenge, rebirth or hidden-identity plot: Micro drama;
- a song, "MV", "to this kind of music": Music video;
- "coming soon", a teaser, a launch or release date: Trailer;
- a birthday, a wedding, an invitation, a greeting, a tribute, a name, "for my mum": Occasion;
- POV, a trend, "for Reels", made to be sent to a friend: Social post;
- a loop, a mood, an effect, an intro: Visual piece;
- characters in a situation, or nothing else to go on: Short film.

A song outranks every other cue, and so does a series. Say the format you read in one line of your reply. Ask only when two formats would give different films and the brief has no cue.

**Where it runs is a separate setting.** A feed is vertical, its first frame is the hook and it must work without sound, whatever the format. A cinema, a website or a television is widescreen and can open more slowly.

**Limits that shape every format.** No lip-synced speech: voice-over and captions carry words, and a look carries a line. No readable text inside pictures: captions only. Music is generated, so nothing can be cut to a beat you cannot hear. Nobody's real face, voice or words unless the person supplied them.

Each structure lists its blocks in order and **turn** marks the fulcrum, as the aim shapes do. Use one, bend one or build your own, and say which in the take's `basis`.

## Short film

A complete small story about a character. 15–90 seconds. Opens late, on a character already in a situation. Ends resolved or reframed. Unit: the turn, and one turn only under a minute. Twenty seconds hold one pair of images and one turn; sixty hold one want, one obstacle, one turn and a breath after it; three minutes hold a failed attempt and a second character's choice. A mood without a turn is a vignette.

- **Single turn.** A calm situation; a detail grows wrong; **turn:** one fact reverses it; stay with the new meaning.
- **Told by its ending.** Ordinary actions, quietly odd; **turn:** the last shot, wider or closer, recasts them. Bend: it reframes the character, not the plot.
- **One want.** The want; an attempt fails; **turn:** a choice or a cost; they get it, or something else. Bend: they get it and it is wrong.
- **Overture and response.** Distance between two; one offers; **turn:** the answer, in its own shot; a held last picture.
- **Loop.** A picture; events; **turn:** the same picture again, read differently. Reuse the clip. Bend: the loop breaks on the last pass.
- **Story in objects.** Four things, each a stage of a life; **turn:** the one that is missing or changed.
- **Fable.** A world with one rule; **turn:** the breach; the moral shown, never said.

## Micro drama

竖屏短剧: serial melodrama. Here, one self-contained piece of 30–90 seconds in its manner. Always vertical, faces filling the frame. The first three seconds are at full intensity: a public humiliation, a mystery, a fall from a height. A reversal about every fifteen seconds. Lines of ten characters or fewer, as captions or one cold line of inner voice-over; looks, a proof object and reactions do the rest. The viewer knows more than the room. Unit: the beat that shifts power. Ends on a button: stop as the door opens, then answer it within three seconds when the piece must stand alone. Conflict comes before logic, and the fresh version subverts the trope from inside it. The usual aim is Payback.

- **Humiliation, slap-back.** The hero is shamed in public; contempt mounts; **turn:** the proof appears; the faces change; a new threat.
- **Hidden identity.** A nobody, treated as nothing; a still shows the viewer the secret; **turn:** someone powerful arrives and bows; the villain's frozen face. Bend: the nobody was the kind one and the boss a fraud.
- **Second life.** A betrayal; a hard cut to the morning it began; **turn:** this time she refuses; the betrayer's mask slips. Reuse the first clip for the second pass.
- **Proof object.** An accusation we believe; an insert of an object; **turn:** it is opened, and read on a face, never on the page; the consequence. Bend: the proof is real and means the opposite.
- **Marry first, love later.** A cold arrangement; a small care only we see; **turn:** the other one sees it; both pretend not to.
- **Door just opening.** The stakes; the approach, in reaction cuts; **turn:** the door opens; cut out.

## Narrated story

One narrator tells a tale and the pictures illustrate it: bedtime, horror, myth, parable, story time. 30–90 seconds. The first sentence is the hook and starts mid-story. About 2.4 words a second: 70 words in 30 seconds, 210 in 90. Unit: the sentence, one picture for each 8–15 words, and the picture shows what the words leave out. Thirty seconds hold one turn; ninety hold a chain of causes and a pattern of three. Music stays low and drops out at the turn. Ends on a moral, a last image, or one line after the turn.

- **First-line hook.** The strangest fact, said first; the ordinary world; it escalates; **turn:** the fact explained, or turned.
- **Story spine.** Once; every day; but one day; because of that, three times; **turn:** until finally; ever since.
- **Three tries.** A want and a refrain; two tries fail; **turn:** the third, done differently; the refrain, changed. For bedtime: slower, with a warm close.
- **Fable.** A flaw; a choice; **turn:** the consequence; one line of moral. Bend: a character says the moral, or it is inverted.
- **Ordinary detail.** A calm routine; the detail again, slightly off; **turn:** the realisation, over black or a freeze; one more line with no picture.
- **Countdown.** Items in rising strangeness; **turn:** the last one reframes the list.
- **Frame story.** An object found; the tale inside it; **turn:** the object again, now meaning something else.
- **Unreliable narrator.** Calm claims over matching pictures; the pictures drift from the words; **turn:** one picture proves the voice wrong; the voice carries on.

## Advert

Success is a sale, a sign-up or a remembered name. 6, 15 or 30 seconds. The first three seconds give the proposition or the problem, with the product or a cue of it. Cues of the brand (a colour, a shape, a sound) come early and return; they are not saved for the end. One claim, said and shown together. The picture carries the proof; never show results, prices or customers that are not real; the product is never the butt of a joke. Unit: a claim and its proof. No readable logo can be generated, so end on a caption with the name, the product held in its light, the voice saying the name and the action, or two clean seconds left for a logo.

- **Hook, product, close.** A hook; **turn:** the product in use, by five seconds; one or two benefits; the action.
- **Problem, solution.** A pain the viewer recognises; **turn:** the product; proof; the offer. Bend: the problem shown as weather or scale.
- **Demonstration.** A claim the viewer doubts; the stakes; **turn:** the proof shot, slow and held; the line. Prefer proofs of scale, light or time to mechanics.
- **Before, after.** The framing before; the product's moment; **turn:** the same framing after; the line. Bend: a modest, specific change.
- **Testimony.** A first-person voice over hands and product; their problem; **turn:** the moment it worked; the recommendation. Never a real person's words unless supplied. Bend: the witness is an object or a pet.
- **Slice of life.** An ordinary scene; a small need; **turn:** the product enters; a warm close. Bend: an odd hour.
- **Bumper.** Six seconds: one image, one line, the name.

## Brand film

Builds a feeling about a brand, a cause or a place of work without a hard sell. 30–90 seconds. Opens on a human moment or a striking picture; the name can wait, but one cue of it runs through. Unit: the beat. The usual aims are Inspire, Moved and Concern.

- **Manifesto.** A provocation in voice-over; pictures build; **turn:** the line that turns it; the name.
- **Origin.** How it was; the trouble; **turn:** what they did; what it means now.
- **The small story.** One person, one day, the brand barely there; **turn:** the moment it mattered; the name, quietly.
- **Cause film.** The harm, made concrete and personal; **turn:** the picture that reaches the viewer; one thing they can do.

## Showcase

Shows off an object, a dish, a garment or a place. No claim, and no story that buries the subject. 5–30 seconds, often a loop. The first seconds show the subject at its best. Fidelity first: condition every picture on the person's photos; move the light and the camera, not the subject; never add features, rooms or views that do not exist. Unit: a facet. One feeling word guides light and pace. The usual aims are Crave and Calm.

- **Hero, detail, context, hero.** The whole; its distinctive details; it in use or in its world; **turn:** the whole again, now known.
- **Walk through.** The outside; the signature room or view; details; the place in use; a closing view.
- **Lookbook.** The full look; fabric moving, slowly; details of cut; the look in a place.

## Social post

Made for a feed: watched to the end, watched again, sent to a friend. 7–30 seconds, vertical, the subject in the middle band. Frame one is the hook; a caption may carry it. No product and no call to action. The game is clear by three seconds; the payoff lands about two thirds in; the end loops to frame one, twists once more or asks a question. Unit: the beat. Copy a trend's structure, never its assets. There is no real creator and no trending sound here: voice-over over pictures stands in for talk to camera.

- **Promise, payoff.** A caption promises a moment; delay; **turn:** the payoff, slowed; a tag, or a loop.
- **POV.** A caption casts the viewer ("POV: you are the last passenger"); the camera is their eyes; **turn:** something addresses them; out. Bend: an impossible point of view.
- **Reversal skit.** The viewer believes one thing; a planted clue; **turn:** it was wrong; a tag. Without the plant there is no twist.
- **Three things.** The promise; three items, a caption number on each; **turn:** the best one last; a question. Bend: the last contradicts the first.
- **Before, after.** A dull state; **turn:** a hard cut; the changed state, held.
- **Day montage.** A morning anchor; four to eight short beats; **turn:** the beat that breaks the pattern; night, or back to the start.
- **Seamless loop.** Start in mid-action; a process; **turn:** its completion, which is the first frame.
- **Slideshow.** The strongest still; six to twelve more, about a second each; **turn:** the odd one out; the last one held. Bend: in order they tell a story.

## Music video

Pictures for a piece of music. 15–30 seconds for a hook clip, longer for a section or a whole song. Opens on its one idea. No speech; text only as a title. The song is generated here, and you know its length and what you asked for, not where its beats fall. So ask for a round tempo (at 120 BPM a bar is two seconds), plan a change of picture material every 8 or 16 seconds, and make each change tolerant: a change of light, a hold, never a hard cut meant for a downbeat. Unit: the section. What works: a concept, a journey, a mood story, one place with one change, dance told in stills and changes of speed. Do not attempt singing to camera, words timed to a vocal, choreography, or anything "on each kick".

- **Rule that escalates.** One impossible rule; more of it; **turn:** at the chorus it doubles in scale; it inverts; the largest picture, held.
- **One place, one change.** A fixed frame, calm; a condition begins to rise (water, light, snow); **turn:** it is total, and held; release.
- **Journey.** Departure, sparse; the land thickens; **turn:** a new kind of landscape; darkness; arrival.
- **Stillness into dance.** A still figure in a dull place; small movements; **turn:** the whole space; one impossible move; back in the chair.
- **Mood fragments.** Who and where; what they want; **turn:** the loss; one picture of afterwards.
- **Hook clip.** Strange from frame one; it develops; **turn:** the reveal; the last frame matches the first.

## Explainer

Success is that the viewer understands something or can do it. 15–30 seconds for one idea, 30–90 for a product or a process. The first three seconds pose the question, or show the problem as a concrete picture; never open on a definition. Voice-over carries the logic at about 2.4 words a second; each line plays over the picture it is about; a few keyword captions signal and do not transcribe. Concrete before abstract. Cut whatever is not the point. Unit: the step of understanding, one a picture. Not for this tool: numbers, charts, labelled diagrams, interface screens, exact hand procedures. The usual aim is Understand.

- **And, but, therefore.** What we know; **turn:** the snag; what follows.
- **Problem, cause, fix.** A symptom the viewer knows; **turn:** its hidden cause, as a picture of scale or of the inside; the fix.
- **Three stages.** The question; stage one; **turn:** stage two, the one nobody expects; stage three; the whole again, fast.
- **One metaphor.** The topic as one physical world; the mapping, said once; **turn:** pushed to where it surprises; where the metaphor stops.
- **Myth, then fact.** The belief, shown vividly; **turn:** a freeze and "actually"; the real picture; why the myth lasts.
- **Question, then reveal.** A concrete puzzle; wrong guesses; a clue; **turn:** the answer, held.
- **How-to.** The finished result; each step as its result, not as a hand motion; the result again.
- **What if.** A premise; a consequence; a bigger one; **turn:** the far one; back to real life.

## Mini documentary

A short true piece about a maker, a place, an event or a fact. 30–90 seconds. Opens on the strongest human moment or the surprising fact, never on background. Every generated picture is an illustration, not a record: keep an illustrated look, say so once in a caption, never invent a face, a quote or an event, and hold the person's real photos as stills without animating them. Voice-over runs over hands, tools, places, weather and objects. Names, dates and figures stay exactly as supplied. Unit: a fact and its picture. Ends on why it matters.

- **And, but, therefore.** The world as it was; **turn:** the trouble; what they did; a closing picture.
- **One true sentence.** The claim; three depictions of it; **turn:** why it matters.
- **A day with the maker.** The workshop at dawn; the steps, by hands and tools; **turn:** the finished thing in use; the workshop empty at night.
- **Object and its story.** An object, unexplained; where it has been; **turn:** who holds it now.
- **Question, surprising answer.** The question; the obvious answer; **turn:** "but actually"; what follows.
- **Recap with one peak.** Arrival; the build; **turn:** the peak, slowed and frozen; afterwards.

## Trailer

Sells something longer that is not in the video: a film, a game, a series, a book, an event, a launch. 15–90 seconds. Hint and never reveal: no ending, no solution. The strongest picture goes just before the title. Cutting quickens toward the peak, with a beat of silence or a held picture before the title. The title and the date are captions over a clean frame. Unit: the escalating hint. When it promotes a real work, use the person's stills and invent nothing the work does not contain.

- **Teaser.** One image or one question; hold; the title.
- **Three steps.** A hook never seen before; the world and its question; three rising steps; **turn:** a hard stop of silence; the strongest picture; the title; a button that undercuts or answers.
- **Cold open.** A whole small scene with no context; **turn:** it breaks off; the title; the world in fast pieces.
- **Pastiche.** The trailer grammar of a genre, recognisable within two cuts; **turn:** the mismatched subject is revealed; the cast parade; a deadpan button.

## Occasion

Marks a moment for named people: a birthday, a wedding, an invitation, a greeting, a tribute, a thank-you. 15–60 seconds. The first three seconds give one specific detail of the person, not a title card. Unit: the remembered detail, a photo or a stated fact held one to three seconds. Stay specific without inventing: show only what the person told you and vary the light and the season, not the facts; from one sentence ("she likes growing flowers"), film the things and no face. Words in a letter must be the person's own. Their photos are held as stills, never animated. Ends on the name, the date or one held picture. Sweetness in the bad sense is a line that explains the feeling, a stock closing phrase or swelling music: cut all three and let one funny detail in.

- **Memory montage.** One thread through the photos; it repeats; **turn:** the unexpected or funny one; a held still and the name.
- **Then and now.** Then; now, in the same framing; more pairs; **turn:** the pair that breaks the pattern.
- **Letter read aloud.** An address and one small shared detail; what I saw you do; **turn:** what I never said; the name.
- **Things they love.** One loved thing; a second, a third; **turn:** together they are the person; the name.
- **Countdown to the date.** Something is being prepared; it builds, with ticks; **turn:** the date, large, over a freeze; the place and one action.
- **Gift being made.** Hands at work, the purpose unclear; it takes shape; **turn:** the finished thing and whom it is for; hold.
- **Year in twelve.** A picture a month, a second each; **turn:** the month held longer; December; the caption.
- **Quiet close.** They were here, in warm detail; humour; **turn:** the music drops; an ordinary empty picture, held, with the name.

## Visual piece

A mood, a motion or an effect for its own sake: a loop, a wallpaper, an intro. 5–20 seconds. No speech. The first frame is already the thing, and it ends where it began. Unit: the motif. The usual aims are Wonder, Calm and Satisfying.

- **Living still.** A picture that seems still; **turn:** one thing in it moves; it settles into a rhythm.
- **One transformation.** A state; **turn:** it becomes another; hold.
- **Motif, three ways.** A shape or a colour; again, elsewhere; **turn:** again, where it means something.
