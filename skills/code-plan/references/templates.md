# Templates

Shapes that people who make each kind of short film keep returning to. They are starting points. Use one, bend one, cross two, or leave them all when the brief gives you something better, and say which in each take (`basis`: "template: <name>", "bent: <name>" or "own"). At least one of your three takes owes nothing to this file. Used straight, every template is somebody's cliché; to bend one, change who does it, where the turn falls or what the last picture is.

Each line lists its blocks in order, and **turn** marks the fulcrum. The timeline only cuts: no dissolves, wipes, split screens or reversed clips.

## Moves for any kind

- A thing used or treated as another thing.
- A framing that lets the viewer assume one thing until a wider or later picture shows another.
- The last character you would expect to do this; power given to the one who should not have it.
- Another kind of film played completely straight: a trailer, a nature documentary or a heist, for something small.
- A phrase or idiom happening for real.
- A shift of scale: the tiny treated as vast, the vast as tiny.
- The same frame at another time, or one thing multiplied.
- One object that carries the film and comes back changed.
- An image that seems unrelated until the ending links it.
- The viewer knowing what the character does not.
- The last picture cutting back to the first, which now means something else.

## Laugh

Gag comedy runs on one mechanism and lives at the cut. Character comedy runs on someone who wants something and keeps their dignity, and lives in reactions. Good, not generic: everyone in the film takes it seriously; exaggerate, since "normal speed" erases a joke; one mechanism only; harmless, more humiliated than hurt; the result registers on a held face or picture.

- **Switch image.** A tight framing reads as grief, danger or romance; hold until it is believed; **turn:** a wider picture shows the innocent truth. Stale: "it was a dream". Bend: reframe the reframe.
- **Wrong solution, in earnest.** A small problem; the character fixes it the hard, absurd way with full concentration; **turn:** the result, and a face that finds it reasonable.
- **Three, then the break.** A pattern in one framing, three times, each a little bigger (reuse the clip, punch in); **turn:** the fourth breaks it. Bend: the fourth is smaller, or nothing happens.
- **Dignity against the world.** A composed character attempts one small proud task; the world defeats it on a cut or by a sound; **turn:** the face that does not move; they carry on. Bend: they win by accident and accept the credit gravely.
- **Animal with a plan.** The animal wants one thing; a polite attempt; a bolder one; **turn:** its ruthless solution, told by the result and the human's reaction. The animal keeps the dignity.
- **Grand build, small payoff.** Epic light, scale and music promise an event; it builds; **turn:** the thing is tiny or ordinary, and the music stops.
- **Exit, changed return.** The character leaves a locked frame with purpose; the empty frame holds over a sound; **turn:** they come back soaked, chased or carrying something. Bend: three returns, each calmer.
- **Look, realise, drop.** Confident action; a freeze as the character notices; a held look; **turn:** a cut to the small, distant result.

## Tension

Suspense tells the viewer and makes them wait. A twist withholds and reframes at the end. They open in opposite ways, so choose. Good, not generic: one firm rule for the threat; empty space and silence in place of a sting; stop early.

- **Bomb under the table.** Show the danger; the character does something ordinary, slowly; cut back to the danger, closer each time; **turn:** the character almost notices. Bend: the threat is harmless and still escalates.
- **Rule of the threat.** Show when it appears (only in the dark, only in reflections); repeat until the viewer has learned it; **turn:** the condition returns, nearer; the rule breaks. One place in two states of light.
- **Thing in the background.** A calm wide picture; a cutaway; **turn:** the same picture with one thing added or missing; hold while the viewer searches.
- **Ordinary turned wrong.** A warm routine; small details gather; **turn:** one last readable picture recasts them. Stale: "dead all along". Bend: a figure of speech made literal.
- **Threat by sound.** Quiet; one sound; a listening face, then the empty doorway; **turn:** the sound again, nearer; cut out. Bend: the sound is a pleasant one.
- **Stop before the answer.** Something inexplicable; a slow approach; **turn:** cut a beat before it is seen, or on a clue that changes the question. The question must be specific.

## Feel

Warmth ends on a reward the viewer sees. Loss ends on something withheld, often an empty frame. Choose first. Good, not generic: feeling the film has earned through one specific act or object; cut away before the tears; leave one thing open. Stale everywhere: an underdog, slow motion and a neat reward.

- **Ritual with one change.** A small ritual in one framing; again, later (the light, the season); **turn:** again, with one person missing or the roles swapped. Bend: the change is a happy one.
- **The misread wish.** A character waits, impatient, and we read it as selfish; it grows; **turn:** they give the thing away.
- **Kindness nobody sees.** A small unrewarded act; repeated; **turn:** what grew from it, shown without the character. End on one result, not three.
- **Object for the person.** An ordinary object, seen with care; someone handles it; **turn:** the object where it tells us its owner is gone. Bend: it is still in use, as when two cups are set out from habit.
- **Reaching the lonely one.** Distance between two; attempts fail; **turn:** the gesture arrives, seen on the receiver's face. Bend: the giver is lonely too.
- **A life in traces.** Things left behind tell it (tickets, a ring box, a crib); they gather; **turn:** one last trace jumps years.

## Calm

Nothing has to happen, but something has to change. Good, not merely pretty: two images that answer each other, one small change, and a pause placed on purpose. A list of nice shots is not a film.

- **Two images and a cut.** A broad image fixes season and place; **turn:** one small close image that the first recolours; hold. Bend: a contrast of scale or temperature.
- **Four parts, one stranger** (起承转合). A place; it develops; **turn:** an image that seems unrelated; a last picture links it by shape or colour, never by explanation.
- **Pillow sequence.** The outside of a place; closer, empty rooms and things; **turn:** one held object that has become charged. Stills and slow push-ins. Bend: one sign of life enters.
- **A day in one place.** One locked frame: dawn, empty; midday, life at a distance; **turn:** the lamps come on; night, empty again. Bend: a single room, or the day in reverse order.
- **Weather arrives.** Stillness; signs (wind in the grass, the light dims); **turn:** the first drops; full weather, then a pause. Bend: what the weather leaves behind.
- **Trace of someone.** The camera stays outside the event; a thing left behind (a wet umbrella, a warm cup); the place without them; **turn:** the first picture again, now meaning more.

## Wonder

Spectacle builds by accumulation and needs no twist. The surreal breaks one rule of the world and needs a turn. Good, not generic: an idea sayable in two lines; exactly one broken rule, played straight; it looks as if it happened in front of a camera.

- **One impossible rule.** Everyday life, filmed plainly; **turn:** the one wrong thing; the world treats it as normal; a small consequence of the rule. Stale: a surreal pile-up.
- **Scale ladder.** A small human scene; each cut is much wider, or much closer, in a rhythm the viewer learns; **turn:** the scale passes the human; a snap back to the start.
- **Flood of simple things.** A quiet real place; the first few arrive; **turn:** the flood, slow; one small detail, then stillness. Avoid counts and collisions. Bend: an unexpected material, or a sad place.
- **Change in place.** One framing and one composition; after each cut the subject is another thing; the chain quickens; **turn:** it lands on the one that means something.
- **Journey backwards.** A familiar present; the surroundings regress; the travellers change too; **turn:** the oddest end state, then a button. Bend: forwards, into an absurd future.

## Desire

Appetite sells texture and sensation. Aspiration sells a mood with the object held back. Proof shows the object surviving a test. Good, not generic: physical imperfection (condensation, a crack, an unruly splash); a cue of the object, its colour or one part, in the first two seconds; no invented claims and no readable text. End on the object alone in its light, held two to three seconds.

- **Hero in light.** Edge glints in darkness; light and a slow move reveal form and material; **turn:** the whole object reads; a detail, then the object held. Bend: it is lit by something from its own world.
- **Crave sequence.** Macro texture; one action (a pour, a break, steam); **turn:** the inside revealed; the hero plate, still. Cut from before the contact to the opened result, with a sound. Stale: bite and smile. Bend: no person, or only hands.
- **Torture test.** The stakes made visible; the test, in one slow wide shot; **turn:** the instant it should fail and does not, on a cutaway; a calm aftermath. Bend: nature is the torturer.
- **Mood first, object last.** A world or a feeling; it deepens; **turn:** the object appears as its source; the object inside the mood. Seed its colour or shape in the first cut.
- **Benefit as spectacle.** An ordinary place; a phenomenon begins; **turn:** its full scale; a quiet detail. The phenomenon is the benefit, literally.
- **Built before your eyes.** Curious parts; assembly quickens, one action per cut; **turn:** the last piece, and the whole is recognised. Bend: it is made from what it promises.

## Satisfaction

Completion has hands in it; time has none; the destruction of order (a clean cut through, a pour over) satisfies too. Good, not generic: a clear "just right" endpoint; the before and the after in the same framing; a sound on every contact.

- **Raw to finished.** Raw material; three to five steps, each one simple action; **turn:** the step where it first looks like the thing; the finished object in use. Show the state before and after each contact and cut across it. Bend: follow one object's journey.
- **Overhead assembly.** One locked top-down frame; layers appear as stills cut on the beat; **turn:** the money shot (a cut, a pour); the finished thing, held. Bend: open on the finished picture.
- **Ruin to restored.** A wreck, framed tight; fragments of work (sparks, sanding, polish); **turn:** a match cut to the restored thing in the identical framing; it works. Bend: restore a place.
- **Time in one frame.** A locked frame; seasons or growth pass as stills from one composition; **turn:** the peak; return or rest. Bend: one human-scale thing that changes against them.
- **Chain reaction.** A first nudge; links, one clip each, cut on the impact with a sound; **turn:** the improbable link; the last trigger on the last beat, then silence. Bend: natural links such as wind, water, light and animals.

## Energy

Pursuit builds by complications. A rhythm montage is built on the music. Good, not generic: the subject in the centre of every frame, so the eye never hunts; waves with a reset before the biggest, not one ramp; a sound on the cuts that matter.

- **Centre-framed pursuit.** The goal and the threat; the pursuer closes; **turn:** a complication flips the advantage; escape or catch. Motion leads into each cut. Bend: a slow pursuer, such as a tide.
- **Disadvantage and hit.** The hero is weaker; they notice a thing in the place; **turn:** the hit, cut before contact and shown again punched in; the reaction earns the finish.
- **Trailer in waves.** A hook never seen before; the world and its question; **turn:** a hard stop of silence, then the biggest wave; a button that undercuts or answers it.
- **Sample montage.** A process as quick simple actions, each with a sound; the repeats build (reuse clips, speed them up); **turn:** the loop breaks or mutates.
- **Rhyme cuts.** Pairs from different worlds whose motions match across the cut; the pairs quicken; **turn:** a pair that reads as one continuous movement. Bend: species, or eras.

## Formats that add rules

- **Advert:** one claim; the picture carries the proof; never show results, prices or customers that are not real; the product is never the butt of the joke.
- **Social post:** the first frame is the hook, readable as a thumbnail, without spending the payoff; the game is clear by three seconds; the last frame can cut back to the first.
- **Trailer or pastiche:** never spoil the ending; the style must be recognisable within two cuts.
