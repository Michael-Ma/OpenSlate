# Tribute / celebration

Birthday, wedding, anniversary, memorial, retirement, graduation, team thank-you: a film honouring real people, usually built from their photos.

## Purpose

Honour a person, couple or group so that the audience feels recognised and moved. Success: the people in it recognise themselves and their moments; the tone fits the occasion.

## Format

- 1–4 min for a screening at the event; 30–60 s for sharing.
- 16:9 for screens; 9:16 or 1:1 for messaging and social.

## Structure

- **Chronological life arc**: beginnings → growth → key milestones → now → a message or wish.
- **Thematic**: chapters by qualities ("adventurous", "family", "work") each with photo moments.
- **Memorial**: gentle open → life chapters → what they gave others → quiet closing image with name and dates.
- End on a message (a toast, a thank-you, a wish) as finishing text or recorded voice.

## Scenes and shots

- 3 min: 5–7 chapters, 30–40 shots from supplied photos.
- 4–6 s per photo with gentle motion; key photos up to the full generated length.
- Generated transition or chapter images (a road, a sunrise, an empty chair) at most 1 per chapter.

## Pacing

- Warm and unhurried; follow the music. Memorials slower; birthdays and weddings can lift in the final third.

## Must-have shots

- Opening photo that defines the person.
- Childhood/early photo (for life arcs).
- Milestones (wedding, graduation, first job, children).
- Group photos with the people who will watch it.
- A candid, characterful photo.
- Final image: the person now or a favourite portrait, with message.

## What matters most

- Faithfulness to the real people and photos.
- Tone appropriate to the occasion.
- Including the people who matter to the audience.

## Narration, text and music

- Music leads; it sets the tone. Optional recorded messages from friends and family.
- Names, dates and chapter titles as finishing text.

## Common mistakes

- Altering faces or "improving" photos so people look like strangers.
- Too many photos at too fast a pace.
- Mismatched tone (jokes in a memorial without the family's lead).
- Leaving out key people.

## Generation caveats

- Real faces are the subject. Animate supplied photos gently (slow push, pan, parallax, subtle light); avoid prompts that make people move, talk, or smile, because image-to-video changes faces and that is distressing for real loved ones. Inspect every face.
- Ask before animating a deceased person's face; some families find it moving, others find it disturbing. Default: camera motion only.
- Do not generate new images of real people (fake memories); use generation only for non-person transitions and backgrounds.
- Old, low-resolution photos may be reinterpreted by the model; keep motion minimal and compare to the original.
- Text on photos (dates, signs) stays as supplied; do not regenerate.

## Intake questions

1. Who is it for, what is the occasion, and what tone? (Default: warm, celebratory.)
2. Please attach the photos in rough order; mark the must-include ones.
3. Should the people in photos move, or only the camera? (Default: camera motion only.)
4. Song choice, length and where it will be shown? Any messages to include?
