# Explainer

## Purpose

Make one idea, service, product or process understood by someone new to it. Success: after one watch the viewer can restate the problem, how the solution works in two or three steps, and what to do next.

## Format

- 60–90 s is the common sweet spot; 30–60 s for social; up to 2–3 min for complex B2B or educational topics.
- 16:9 for web and YouTube; 9:16 or 1:1 for social versions.

## Structure

- **Problem → solution → how it works → benefits/proof → CTA.** Set up the problem in about 15 s or less.
- Alternative: **PAS** (problem, agitate, solution) when the viewer underestimates the problem.
- "How it works" is 2–4 steps, never more. Each step is one scene.
- Concept explainers (no product): question → intuition → mechanism → example → takeaway.

## Scenes and shots

- 60–90 s: 4–6 scenes, 12–20 shots. One idea per scene.
- 2–4 shots per scene: a framing shot of the idea, a mechanism shot, and an example or result.
- Shot lengths follow the narration sentence: usually 3–6 s.

## Pacing

- Steady and clear. One new idea at a time; let each land before the next.
- Re-use a visual system so the viewer learns it once: the same character, the same icon for the product, the same colour for "problem" vs "solution".

## Must-have shots

- Relatable problem shot: a person or situation the audience recognises.
- A visual metaphor or diagram-like image for the core mechanism.
- One shot per step of "how it works", composed so steps read in sequence (same layout, changing state).
- Result/benefit shot: the life after.
- End frame with space for the CTA.

## What matters most

- Clarity over cleverness. A single metaphor carried through.
- Narration-picture sync: the shot shows the thing being said at that moment.
- Consistent visual language (often illustrated/2D/3D motion; see visual styles).

## Narration, text and music

- Narration-led. Budget about 150 words per minute: 60 s ≈ 150 words, 90 s ≈ 220. Plain language, second person, short sentences.
- Write the script first and plan shots against it; each shot covers one or two sentences.
- Labels, step numbers and key terms as finishing text, not generated. Music low and neutral under voice.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the company history instead of the viewer's problem.
- Too many features or steps.
- Visuals that decorate rather than explain.
- Jargon.
- A script read too fast to fit too many words.

## Generation caveats

- Diagrams, charts, arrows, labels and UI do not render reliably. Compose diagrammatic frames with shapes and space; add labels in finishing. Do not ask the model to draw a correct chart or number.
- Keep a mascot or recurring character locked to a reference.
- Process steps need distinct start states; one state change per shot.
- Abstract metaphors drift; describe the concrete object, its position and its single motion.
- Real software UI belongs to app promo rules: use supplied screenshots, not generated screens.

## Intake questions

1. Who is the audience and what do they already know? (Default: newcomers.)
2. What is the one thing they must understand, and the action you want afterwards?
3. Is there an approved script or recording? If not, I will draft one to about 150 words per minute.
4. Illustrated/animated look, or live-action look? (Default: decided with the visual-style guide.)
