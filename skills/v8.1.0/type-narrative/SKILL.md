# Narrative

Routed for `filmKind.kind: "narrative"`. Read [short film](references/short.md) for `short`, `micro_drama` and `visual_poem`, and [kids' story](references/kids-story.md) for `kids_story` or `audience: "kids"`. Characters follow `craft-characters`. Authority and tools are in core.

## Shape

- A logline: who wants what, and what stands in the way. One turn in a short under 60 s; a mirrored first and last image shows the change.
- Story Spine or a three-act micro structure; each scene has a cause and an effect. Show, do not tell: dialogue is staged off-face or carried by narration, because lip sync is unreliable.
- Keep the cast small (one or two recurring characters) and offer sheets for anyone in more than one shot.

## Subtypes

- `micro_drama` (vertical short drama): a hook in frame 1, a reversal per episode beat, a cliffhanger end; 9:16 framing rules from `format-social-vertical`.
- `kids_story`: simple cause and effect, repetition, warm resolution, no distress or scary scale; narration about 2 words per second.
- `visual_poem`: a rule or mood instead of plot; one motif that returns changed.

## Shots

Must-haves: an establishing image of the world, the character's want, the turn, the climax image, the ending image. Cut between angles rather than long continuous action; one action per clip. Keep wardrobe per scene and identity wording fixed.

## Comedy

A comic short reads `craft-comedy`: the premise method replaces logline brainstorming, and the turn is the punch.

## Story and style

Story unit: the scene. Core: the theme. Frameworks: Story Spine, three-act micro, kishōtenketsu for gentle stories. Avoid two turns in 60 s. Style: `grounded-realism`, `vintage-film-35mm`, `anime-2d`; kids: `pastel-storybook`, `stylized-3d-feature`, `paper-cutout`.

## Intake

What happens in one sentence; who the characters are and any supplied likeness; audience (kids changes pacing and safety); length and aspect when unknown.
